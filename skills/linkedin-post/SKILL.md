# LinkedIn post

Read `post-basics` first. It covers facts, the invented-claims guard, topic mix, timing and the final checklist.

## When to use

A post for a personal profile or a company page. Not for X, Threads or Bluesky: do not paste the same text there, write each one separately.

## Voice

- Personal profile: calm, honest, first person, normal capitalisation and punctuation. Observation, then conclusion, then one concrete fact.
- Company page: "we", never the founder's "I".
- No sarcasm, no "lol", no hashtag block, no punchline ending, no "I'm humbled to announce".
- Do not write a mini-article. A LinkedIn post is one idea.

## Form

- Length: 400-1200 characters is the sweet spot. One-line posts are fine now and then. The platform allows 3,000 characters maximum, but long is rarely better.
- The first two lines decide everything, because the feed cuts a long post behind "see more". Put the point there, not a greeting.
- Short paragraphs, one blank line between them. No walls of text.
- Plain text. Do not use fake bold made of Unicode letters: it breaks search and screen readers.
- Emoji: none or one. No emoji bullets.
- Hashtags: none on a personal voice. If you must, one or two at the end.
- Links: put the link in the post only when it is the point. Do not stuff it in every post.
- Formats to rotate: one observation with a fact, a short numbered how-to, a before and after, a question to readers, a reaction to confirmed news.

## Images and documents

- A post with an image is the default on a company page. A text-only company post is the exception.
- Personal profile: a plain real screenshot on some posts, not all. See `post-basics`, section 6.
- Document (PDF carousel): every slide must be readable on a phone, one idea per slide. The caption may mention the document only if it is attached when the post goes out, and only for what it truly contains.

## Topics

A good LinkedIn topic is something you did, learned or decided, told with a number you can source. If you have no strong number, write the post without one. Competitor or trend posts: at most 1 in 4-5, confirmed sources only.

## Replies

Comment under live posts from people in your niche when you have real experience of the point. Two or three sentences. No "great post!", no pitch.

## Example

Facts given: "Pinecone Shop added saved carts on 3 March. Source: changelog."

> Saved carts shipped in Pinecone Shop this week.
>
> If you sell things people think about for a day before buying, the cart used to be empty when they came back. Now it is waiting.
>
> It is a small change. We built it because the checkout drop-off was where most visitors left.

The last sentence invents a reason, and it is not in the facts. Cut it, or ask the author whether it is true. That is the guard working.
